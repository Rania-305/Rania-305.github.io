# Personal Website Proposal Notes

## Goal
A clean, professional site that shows who I am, what I've built, and how to reach me. A recruiter, hiring manager, or collaborator should understand my story and see my best work within 60 seconds.

## Audience
- Recruiters and hiring managers (strategy, consulting, analytics, tech)
- Founders and collaborators
- Professors, mentors, and my network in Miami's tech and education community

## Pages

### 1. Home
- Name and one-line tagline
- Short intro (2-3 sentences)
- Photo
- Links to featured projects
- Buttons: View My Work and Contact Me

### 2. Projects
Each project gets a card with:
- Title and one-sentence summary
- My role and what I did
- Tools and skills used
- Results or impact (numbers where possible)
- Link, demo, or screenshots

Project ideas to feature:
- AI voice and SMS agent systems built at OutRival AI
- GTM workforce planning work from my Kaseya internship (sanitized, no confidential details)
- Class and personal projects (mobile app, data analytics work)
- Code/Art teaching and program contributions

### 3. Experience
- Timeline of roles: OutRival AI, Kaseya, Liberty City Ventures, Lab22c, Code/Art
- 2-3 bullets per role focused on outcomes, not duties

### 4. About
- My story: Miami roots, path through Code/Art (student to TA to instructor to board member)
- What I care about and what I'm looking for next
- A few personal touches (interests, hobbies)

### 5. Resume
- Embedded PDF plus a download button

### 6. Contact
- Email, LinkedIn, GitHub
- Simple contact form or a mailto link

## Optional Extras
- Writing or blog: short posts on projects and lessons learned
- Testimonials: quotes from managers, professors, or students
- Skills section: Python, SQL, analytics, AI/LLM tooling, strategy

## Design Principles
- Simple, minimal layout with lots of white space
- Fast loading and mobile-friendly
- One accent color and one or two fonts
- Every page has a clear next step

## Tech Options
- Framer / Webflow: fast and polished, no code, but less control and may cost money
- Next.js + Vercel: full control and shows technical skill, but more build time
- GitHub Pages: free and simple, but basic features

Recommendation: Next.js on Vercel if the site itself should be a portfolio piece, or Framer if speed matters more.

## Timeline
- Week 1: Collect content (bio, photo, project write-ups, resume)
- Week 2: Design and build homepage and projects page
- Week 3: Build remaining pages, test on mobile
- Week 4: Launch with a custom domain and share on LinkedIn

## Success Criteria
- Live on a custom domain
- At least 3-4 strong projects with real results
- Linked from my resume and LinkedIn
- Loads in under 3 seconds and looks good on mobile
